# Thin intraday edges die in 0DTE costs

The published evidence supports a few simple, fully mechanical intraday rules on the underlying: the opening range breakout (ORB) on QQQ and on high-relative-volume "stocks in play", the SPY "noise area" momentum breakout with a VWAP trailing stop, and the first-half-hour/last-half-hour market momentum effect. Every one of them shows a positive edge before costs in its own paper, and every independent replication finds that edge small (about +0.07 to +0.13R per trade, Sharpe roughly halved out of sample) and fragile: it is gone at about 2 cents a share of slippage on QQQ, about 2 basis points on SPY, or less than one tick for the VWAP rule. Nothing in the record shows a positive edge from buying 0DTE calls and puts. The academic work on retail 0DTE trading finds steady losses, mostly from transaction costs, and finds 0DTE trades doing about 3% worse than other option trades. ICT/smart money concepts (liquidity sweeps, order blocks, fair value gaps) have no peer-reviewed test at all, and the practitioner tests that exist find nothing beyond index drift, which matches Scalp City's own TJR record (profit factor 0.91–0.97 before option costs). Taken together, the evidence says the bot should first find a signal that makes money on the underlying after realistic costs, and test it with multiple-testing corrections. Only after that is it worth asking whether an option structure can carry the edge. A 65%+ win rate is a design choice, not a sign of skill. It is cheap to reach by moving targets in and stops out, and it says nothing about profitability unless the average win, the average loss and costs come with it.

A note on sourcing: the research sessions behind this report could not open most primary PDFs (SSRN, Concretum, the SEC and Cboe were blocked). Figures come from abstracts, search extracts and GitHub replications that could be read. Anything the bot hard-codes should be checked against the original paper first.

## Four published intraday rules beat buy-and-hold only before realistic costs

The opening range breakout is the best-documented intraday rule for US ETFs. In **"Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (Zarattini & Aziz, 2023)**, the authors trade QQQ from the first 5-minute bar. A bullish 09:30–09:35 bar means buying at the 09:35 open with the stop at that bar's low. A bearish bar means a short with the stop at its high, and a doji means no trade. The exits are the stop (−1R), a +10R target, or the close. Size is the smaller of 1% of equity divided by R per share and 4× leverage ([GitHub replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). From January 2016 to February 2023, starting from $25,000, the paper reports roughly 31–33% CAGR, a Sharpe ratio of about 1.1 and a 22% maximum drawdown. Its only cost is a $0.0005/share commission, and the authors state plainly that "we assumed no slippage in fills" ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy/)). An independent replication matches the paper gross (1,775 vs 1,795 trades, Sharpe 1.06 vs 1.12, the same 22% drawdown). It also shows what the edge is made of. **About 75% of trades stop out, about 22% are held to the close, and the +10R target is hit only 2–3% of the time.** Net profit falls from $138,639 at zero slippage to $4,860 at 2 cents a share, and **to zero at about 2.2 cents** ([GitHub replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). A secondary source puts the hit rate at 24% and the average at +0.13R per trade ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). By my arithmetic, that means winners average about 3.7R. The same MQL5 test on five index CFDs reproduced the gross edge on Nasdaq futures (+0.131R), but after costs none of the markets could be told apart from zero.

A separate QQQ variant was chosen from a 120-configuration sweep on 2017–2022 data. It uses a 15-minute range, enters on a 5-minute close beyond it, takes a 2R target, stops entering at 12:00 and exits at 15:00. It is the cleanest out-of-sample number in the set. From January 2023 to September 2026 it made 928 trades with **46.7% wins, +0.070R per trade and a profit factor of 1.156 before costs**, then lost 7.7R in 2026. At $0.04 round trip, the bootstrap confidence interval includes zero ([mericck, GitHub](https://github.com/mericck/QQQ-Opening-Range-Breakout)). The "Stocks in Play" version **(Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, 2024)** filters more than 7,000 US stocks to those that open above $5, average at least 1M shares a day and have ATR(14) above $0.50. It then keeps the top 20 by opening relative volume (first-5-minute volume against the same window over 14 days, at least 100%). Entry is a stop order at the opening-range high or low in the direction of the first candle, with a stop 10% of ATR away and an exit at the close ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/); [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)). It reports more than 1,600% net and a **Sharpe of 2.81 for 2016–2023**, with a $0.0035/share commission and no spread or slippage ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/); [Tradoki](https://blog.tradoki.com/posts/opening-range-breakout-stocks-in-play-research)). A QuantConnect recreation got a Sharpe of about 2.4. No independent test after 2023 was found. On a stock with a $0.50 ATR the stop is about 5 cents away, so a 1–2 cent spread is a large fraction of 1R. These are the conditions under which backtests overstate fills most.

Intraday momentum on SPY has the strongest academic standing. **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018)** show that SPY's first-half-hour return, measured from the prior close, predicts the last-half-hour return over 1993–2013. The R² is about 1.6%, rising to 3.3% when combined with the 12th half-hour on high-volatility days. The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and macro-news days. The trading rule takes the sign of the first-half-hour return and holds SPY over the last 30 minutes ([SSRN 2440866](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)). It appears in 12 of 16 international markets ([Li et al., Reading](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)). Later evidence is thin, though: one non-peer-reviewed check of 1,085 SPX sessions in the 0DTE era finds the unconditional effect flat in every year ([firmtape, DEV](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)).

**"Beat the Market" (Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, Swiss Finance Institute WP 24-97)** turns this into a tradable system. For each minute of the day, sigma is the 14-day average of the absolute move from the open to that minute. The upper band is max(open, prior close) × (1 + sigma) and the lower band is min(open, prior close) × (1 − sigma). The rule checks only at :00 and :30 from 10:00 to 15:30. It goes long above the upper band and short below the lower band, trails the stop at max(VWAP, band) for longs and min(VWAP, band) for shorts, and is flat by 16:00. Leverage is min(4, 2% ÷ SPY's 14-day daily volatility) ([svettelnova replication](https://github.com/svettelnova/intraday-momentum-spy); [nikolaosntinas18 replication](https://github.com/nikolaosntinas18/spy-intraday-momentum)). The paper reports **1,985% total net, 19.6% a year, Sharpe 1.33 and a 25% maximum drawdown for 2007 to early 2024**, assuming $0.0035/share commission plus $0.001/share slippage ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/beat-the-market-with-intraday-momentum)). The tighter VWAP stop did most of the work, lifting the Sharpe from 0.61 to 1.24. Independent work is sobering. One replication finds an **out-of-sample Sharpe of 0.75 for 2022–September 2026, against 0.67 for buy-and-hold SPY**, inside an uncertainty band of about ±0.4–0.5 ([nikolaosntinas18](https://github.com/nikolaosntinas18/spy-intraday-momentum)). Another (seen only as a search extract) reports a Sharpe near zero since 2025 and says walk-forward "destroys value" ([codecat-ops](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)). A 58-backtest cost study puts **break-even at about 2.1–2.4 basis points of extra slippage per trade**. It also finds the strategy's real merit is a beta near zero and a 9% drawdown, not alpha over volatility-matched SPY ([francesco-nicolo](https://github.com/francesco-nicolo/intraday-momentum-replication)).

The **VWAP rule (Zarattini & Aziz, 2023, "VWAP: The Holy Grail for Day Trading Systems")** is long above VWAP and short below it on QQQ 1-minute bars. For January 2018–September 2023 it reports 671% net of commissions, a Sharpe of 2.1 and a 9.4% drawdown, under the assumption of "no slippage in our order fills" ([SSRN 4631351](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4631351); [Telonics](https://telonicstrading.com/research-vwap.html)). A reproduction on six years of 1-minute data finds **the edge per trade is smaller than one tick of slippage** ([Telonics](https://telonicstrading.com/research-vwap.html)). One more oddity is worth checking in the PDFs: the VWAP paper's final equity of $192,656 is almost identical to the ORB paper's $192,806.

| Method | Core rule | Sample | Costs assumed | Reported | Independent result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QQQ 5-min ORB | Direction of 09:30–09:35 bar; stop at its other end; hold to close | 2016–Feb 2023 | $0.0005/sh, no slippage | Sharpe ~1.1, CAGR ~31%, DD 22% | Gross matched; edge zero at ~2.2¢/sh slippage; 75% stop-outs |
| QQQ 15-min ORB (sweep-selected) | 5-min close beyond 15-min range, 2R target, entries before 12:00 | OOS Jan 2023–Sep 2026 | $0.04 round trip tested | PF 1.156, 46.7% wins, +0.07R pre-cost | CI includes zero after costs; −7.7R in 2026 |
| Stocks in Play ORB | Top 20 relative volume; stop entry at OR; stop 10% ATR | 2016–2023 | $0.0035/sh only | Sharpe 2.81, >1,600% | QuantConnect Sharpe ~2.4; no post-2023 test |

The common thread is that the authors, whose firm (Concretum, with Bear Bull Traders) has a commercial interest in these results, assume little or no slippage, while every independent tester with costs and newer data finds the Sharpe roughly halved or gone. The paper figures are best read as upper bounds. Older academic work agrees on the conditioning variable: ORB returns on S&P 500 futures differ by about 150 basis points a day between the highest and lowest volatility states ([Lundström, SWoPEc](https://swopec.hhs.se/umnees/abs/umnees0974.htm)). The ORB replication's profits are similarly concentrated, with 76% of one filter's P&L coming from 2022 ([giovannibrusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). Common retail setups such as gap fills, prior-day high/low breaks and first-hour reversals have no peer-reviewed, cost-inclusive backtest. The gap-fill statistics that do exist show same-day fills of 0.25%+ SPY up-gaps falling from **58.1% in 2001–2013 to 43.1% in 2014–2026** ([Trade That Swing](https://tradethatswing.com/sp-500-spy-es-gap-fill-strategy-and-statistics/)). A fill rate is not a return, either, because it says nothing about what is lost when the gap does not fill ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/gap-trading-strategies/)).

## Buying 0DTE options turns a thin underlying edge into a losing trade

None of the sources reviewed finds a positive expected value from buying at-the-money or out-of-the-money 0DTE options. **Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda ("Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?", SSRN 4404704)** identify retail SPX trades through Cboe's price-improvement auctions. Retail loses about **$241,000 on an average day from February 2021 to September 2023, and about $350,000 a day once daily expiries began**. The losses concentrate in single-leg, debit and high-implied-volatility trades. That is exactly what a directional bot buying calls and puts does ([College Sidekick copy of the SSRN PDF](https://www.collegesidekick.com/study-docs/2487577); [Bloomberg via BNN](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)). Summaries attribute **roughly 50–60% of the losses to transaction costs** rather than to getting the direction wrong ([Neudata](https://neudata.co/literature-reviews/zero-day-to-expiry-options-a-losing-bet-for-retail-traders); [Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/zero-days-to-expiration-options-investment-or-gambling/)). **Bogousslavsky & Muravyev** study about $15B of retail option trades from 2020–2022. They find an average return of −0.9%, and **0DTE trades earn about 3% less than other option trades** ([LSU-hosted PDF](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)). One counterpoint is a Cboe-hosted study finding Cboe customers' simple trades profitable on average. It is not 0DTE-specific, and it is published by the exchange that sells the product ([Cboe PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)).

The pricing side shows why. **Vilkov ("0DTE Trading Rules", SPXW September 2016–January 2026, entries at 10:00 held to the close)** finds a positive but tiny 0DTE variance risk premium: a median of about 0.0011% of the index from 10:00 to expiry. Buyers therefore overpay only slightly on average. Even so, "ATM-to-OTM calls and most puts lose on average", individual returns are hugely volatile, and paying half the spread instead of trading at mid "mechanically lowers mean PNLs and annualized Sharpe ratios for most strategies." The paper concludes that "unconditional 0DTE exposure is difficult to justify" ([Vilkov, GitHub](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/blob/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md)). Deep in-the-money calls look best, partly because the S&P 500 drifted up over the sample. Costs scale with time to expiry: the average relative spread runs from **about 9% for 0DTE to about 2% for 21–48 DTE** S&P options ([Doshi & Pederzoli, JFQA](https://paolapederzoli.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/risky_order_flow_jfqa.pdf)). Short-dated retail-traded options average 12.6% quoted spreads, and 28.7% when out of the money ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13285)). The main mitigation the research identifies is execution. Customers who use non-marketable limit orders in active, slightly-OTM 0DTE series sit at the best bid or offer more than half the time, and their costs are low ([SEC DERA, "Hope at a Reasonable Price"](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-economic-risk-analysis/staff-papers-analyses/hope-reasonable-price_customer-use-limit-orders-0dte-market)).

Rough arithmetic makes the problem concrete for Scalp City. Using the standard at-the-money approximation, a SPY $580 at-the-money 0DTE call bought at 10:00 with 15% implied volatility is worth about $2.11. The penny-wide SPY spread is cheap, about 1% of premium round trip. Theta is not: with SPY flat, the call loses about 4% of its value in 30 minutes and about 9% in an hour, **so the shipped −10% premium stop fires from time decay alone within about an hour**, or on an adverse SPY move of about 0.07%. In the last hour the decay is several times faster, and quoted spreads of $0.15–0.40 in the final 15 minutes have been reported ([TradingBlock](https://www.tradingblock.com/blog/0dte-spy-vs-spx-options)). These are illustrative estimates, not measured fills. They do explain the repo's corrected baseline, in which 92 premium stops lost $8,324 against 21 targets that made $2,508. The ORB evidence adds a structural mismatch: ORB's profits come from the hold-to-close leg, and holding to the close is where a 0DTE option bleeds most. The sound inference is that a directional signal should be judged on the underlying first, where there is no theta, no volatility premium and a one-cent spread. **If it loses there, as the TJR record does at PF 0.91–0.97, no option wrapper can rescue it.** If an edge is found, the evidence favours carrying it with shares, higher-delta (about 0.6–0.8) or 1–7 DTE options, entered with limit orders, and with stops set in underlying terms.

## ICT and smart money concepts have no peer-reviewed support, and replications find only drift

The research found no peer-reviewed, SSRN or arXiv study that tests fair value gaps, order blocks, liquidity sweeps, market structure shifts or the Silver Bullet window as trading rules. The most systematic practitioner test is **StatOasis's 648 backtests** of order blocks, FVGs, sweeps and optimal trade entry on SPY, QQQ, DIA and IWM. Each was compared with a seeded coin flip and with buy-and-hold. **None showed a significant edge. The best was order blocks on SPY at +0.121% over 5 days across 736 events, t = 1.22, and 0 of 648 beat buy-and-hold** ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)). The test used daily bars, so it does not settle the intraday question, but its pattern (profits that are only index drift, with filters adding nothing) is exactly what Scalp City measured. The repo's 2,090 TJR setups won 22% at −0.011R (PF 0.97), and the 208 setups the plan would actually take did worse at −0.039R (PF 0.91). The one public mechanical Silver Bullet backtest on NQ/ES lost: 27 NQ trades, 25.9% wins, PF 0.51, −19.87R over 72 base-spec trades, and the best variant was negative out of sample ([GitHub silver-bullet-backtest](https://github.com/cjosh4toyotas-stack/silver-bullet-backtest)). A random-walk simulation found bullish FVGs revisited within 20 bars at **83.4%, against 83.5% for random price levels**. That suggests the popular "FVGs get filled" statistic is just geometry ([Thortradecopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). Promotional claims of 55–80% win rates come with no method or sample size ([Lune](https://lunefi.com/blog/ict-trading-strategy-2026-65-75-win-rates-prop-firm)). "Price tags the prior-day high 75–88% of the time" statistics measure touches, not trade payoffs ([Kieran Duff](https://kieranduff.substack.com/p/68-percent-isnt-an-edge); [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/inside-day-in-trading-breakout-data)).

The academic kernel under ICT is real, but it cuts both ways. Osler's analysis of a bank's FX order book (9,655 orders, more than $55bn) shows **take-profits clustering at round numbers, which produces reversals, and stop-losses clustering just beyond them, which produces self-reinforcing cascades once the level is crossed** ([NY Fed SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf); [NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). US stocks show net buying just below and net selling just above whole-dollar prices across more than 100 million trades ([Bhattacharya, Holden & Jacobsen, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1364)). Statistically detected support and resistance levels bounce more often than chance, and more so after repeated touches ([Chung & Bellotti, arXiv](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2101.07410.html); [Garzarelli et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1110.5197)). None of these studies reports a trading P&L after costs. Osler's cascade finding also predicts **continuation** through swept stops at least as strongly as reversal, and the strongest published intraday SPY evidence (Gao et al. and Beat the Market) is trend-following. A sweep-then-reverse rule that bets against the cascade has no published backing. The honest experiment is to score both directions after a sweep against a random-level baseline.

## A credible edge must clear multiple-testing hurdles the project has not yet met

Backtest statistics set a higher bar than a positive backtest. **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu ("Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism", Notices of the AMS, 2014)** show that after only 7 independent configurations, a researcher expects to find a 2-year backtest with a Sharpe above 1 even when the true Sharpe is zero. With 5 years of data, no more than 45 configurations should be tried ([Carma PDF](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). Their **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** asks whether the winner beats the expected maximum Sharpe of N noise strategies, adjusting for skew and kurtosis ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). The **Probability of Backtest Overfitting** uses combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV): split the days into S blocks, and for every half/half combination check whether the in-sample best variant lands below the median out of sample ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). **Harvey, Liu & Zhu** argue that a new effect found after heavy data mining needs t > 3, not 2 ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Chen counters that this is conservative ([arXiv 2206.15365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15365)). **Harvey & Liu's "Backtesting"** gives a Sharpe haircut that is heavy on marginal strategies and light on strong ones ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). Quantopian's study of 888 algorithms found backtest Sharpe ratios "offer little value in predicting out of sample performance", and that the gap widened the more a strategy had been backtested ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)).

By standard binomial and t-test arithmetic (computed, not quoted), pinning a win rate to ±5 points takes about 250–385 trades. A +0.1R edge with a 1.2R standard deviation needs about 576 trades for t = 2 and 1,296 for t = 3. Under these rules the project's 208 in-plan TJR setups give a 95% interval of roughly −0.24R to +0.17R. That is no detectable edge, in either direction. The research gate's "edge over the last 5 sessions" is a hypothesis test run many times a day with no multiplicity correction. That explains why triggers it "proved" won about 39% going forward. The well-regarded practical sources point the same way. **Davey's *Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems*** lays out the pipeline: walk-forward, a one-shot evaluation against criteria fixed in advance, Monte Carlo reshuffles for drawdown ranges, and 6–12 months of incubation ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about)). **Chan's *Quantitative Trading*** catalogues look-ahead, survivorship and data-snooping bias, and his slide deck is free ([CMT-hosted slides](https://cmtassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/erniechan-020211-1.pdf)). **Carver** covers sizing and robustness, including his "three ways to overfit" talks ([CMT Association](https://cmtassociation.org/?p=17000)). **López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*** contributes purged, embargoed cross-validation, triple-barrier labels (which `edge_tracker` already uses) and meta-labeling, the principled form of a "gate" that sizes or skips a primary signal that already has an edge ([LuxAlgo explainer](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/purged-cross-validation.md)). Georgia Tech's free "Machine Learning for Trading" course exists but reviews are mixed ([Class Central](https://classcentral.com/course/udacity-machine-learning-for-trading-1026)).

## Rules a bot can implement and test now

The evidence supports one testing order. Build the published underlying strategies as controls, put any ICT variant against them on the same days and under the same costs, and touch options only once something survives. The table lists the candidates with the evidence-backed parameters. All are fully mechanical with no look-ahead, and none has an edge established after costs out of sample.

| Candidate | Implementable rule | Why it is worth testing | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| QQQ/SPY 5-min ORB | First bar direction; stop at its other end; hold to 15:55; 1% risk | Published, replicated gross | Stop slippage; needs about <2¢/sh costs |
| 15-min ORB, 2R target | Entry on 5-min close beyond 15-min range, before 12:00 | Only clean out-of-sample record found (PF 1.16 pre-cost) | Negative in 2026 |
| Noise-band momentum (SPY) | Bands from 14-day time-of-day moves, gap-adjusted; checks at :00/:30; VWAP trailing stop | Peer-adjacent paper plus several replications | Decay since 2025; 2 bp break-even |
| First/last half-hour (Gao) | Sign of 09:30-from-prior-close return through 10:00 → hold 15:30–16:00 | JFE-published; one trade a day | Effect may be gone post-2014 |
| Sweep-direction test | Print beyond PDH/PDL or overnight high/low, close back inside within 1–5 bars; score fade **and** continuation at fixed R with 60–120 min time stop vs random levels | Directly tests Osler vs ICT | No prior evidence either way |
| Round-number fade/breakout | Whole-dollar and $5 levels; fade first touch, break after close beyond; split by direction of arrival | Osler; Bhattacharya et al. | Effects small and short-lived |
| FVG baseline | Revisit rate of FVGs vs random levels at matched distance | Cheap falsification test | Likely null, per simulation |

Each candidate should then pass the same validation regime. Fill at the ask to buy and the bid to sell, never at the last trade. Re-run every result with slippage doubled and reject anything that only works at zero slippage. Report trades, win rate with a Wilson interval, average R with a t-statistic computed on daily R sums (SPY, QQQ and IWM signals on the same day are not independent), profit factor and maximum drawdown. Keep a ledger of every variant ever tried, so that N in the Deflated Sharpe calculation is honest. Hold out the most recent 30–40% of days and run that holdout exactly once. As judgment calls built on the cited methods (not published constants), a reasonable bar before paper incubation is n ≥ 100 trades, t(mean R) ≥ 3, a net profit factor above 1.3, a Deflated Sharpe above 0.95 and a PBO below 0.2, with positive expectancy in at least two of three volatility terciles. Kill rules should be fixed in advance from a Monte Carlo of the backtest trades. A strategy that passes on the underlying can then be run through 0.7-delta 0DTE and at-the-money 3–5 DTE contracts with limit orders and stops in underlying terms. That comparison would itself be new evidence, because no published study runs one intraday signal through shares, 0DTE and weeklies side by side. Separately, FINRA's replacement of the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum took effect on 4 June 2026, but brokers have until 20 October 2027 to implement it ([E*TRADE](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change)). The repo's PDT guard should stay until Alpaca confirms its own change.

## A 65% win rate is cheap to manufacture and says nothing about profit

Win rate and profitability are separate dials, linked by payoff. Expectancy per trade is win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss − costs. At a 65% win rate, a strategy breaks even before costs when the average win is about 0.54 of the average loss. Any rule can be pushed above 65% by moving the target closer and the stop further away, and costs then take a larger share of each small win. The published strategies that make money gross do it the other way around. The QQQ ORB wins about 24% of the time and earns +0.13R. The best out-of-sample ORB wins 46.7% and earns +0.07R. Vilkov's data show where high win rates really live in 0DTE: **selling** slightly out-of-the-money options is profitable in up to 75% of observations. The price is a tail, with a 1% expected shortfall of about 0.58–1.58% of the index per day ([Vilkov](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/blob/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md)). A high-win-rate, short-premium book is a different, tail-risk business, not an improved version of the current bot. No source reviewed shows a buyer of 0DTE options, or an ICT model, reaching 65%+ wins together with positive expectancy after costs. The owner's goal is best restated as a statistically significant positive expectancy and a profit factor above about 1.3 after costs on the underlying, reported alongside the win rate rather than replaced by it.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the question from "which setup wins most often" to "which market and instrument leave any edge after costs". The answer is narrow. The only documented intraday edges on US ETFs are trend-following (opening-range and noise-band breakouts, first-to-last half-hour momentum). They pay through rare large winners and held positions, and they survive roughly 2 cents or 2 basis points of friction. That profile is the opposite of what an at-the-money 0DTE option rewards, since the option charges theta for holding and spread for every exit. The TJR record has no edge to lose: it is a reversal-style model with no published support, and it scored break-even to negative before any option cost. The highest-value next step is therefore not another filter on `smc_retrace`. It is to run the published momentum and ORB controls on the same Alpaca data and costs, and a two-sided sweep test against random levels, all under the DSR/PBO discipline.

Two things are still unknown and worth measuring in-repo, because no outside source answers them. The first is the real time-of-day spread on SPY/QQQ 0DTE and single-stock weeklies, which the `data/option_bars/` cache can supply. The second is how one surviving underlying signal performs across shares, 0DTE and 1–7 DTE options. Until a signal clears those tests, the honest message to the owner is that the evidence supports neither a 65% win rate nor profitability for the current design. It does support a clear, falsifiable way to look for an edge.
