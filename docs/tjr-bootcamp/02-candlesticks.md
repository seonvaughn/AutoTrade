# Day 2: Candlesticks
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bEkbNGUzQME (23 min). Read in full from the transcript.

## Concepts
- **A candle = one timeframe's price action.** Body runs open→close; wicks show how far price went without
  closing there ("where price went but never closed"). Green: open at bottom of the body, close at top; red the reverse.
- **Timeframe** is how long a candle takes to close. With practice you can infer lower-timeframe structure from one or
  two higher-timeframe candles.
- **Only single-candle patterns matter**: doji (small body, long wicks), long-legged doji, dragonfly doji, cross/
  inverse cross doji, hammer. He rejects all patterns of 3+ candles ("mountain tops", W's, head and shoulders).
- **Reading wicks** ("wicks don't lie"):
  - A long upper wick on a bullish candle = bulls weren't strong enough to close up there; "more than likely it was
    taking liquidity" above.
  - A doji after a strong move = **indecision / loss of momentum** (e.g. a long lower wick with no strong close after a
    sell-off = bearish momentum fading; price then consolidated).
  - **Strong close** = a full body with small wicks, closing near its extreme: momentum continues.
  - **Strong rejection candle**: long lower wick, closes near its high with a tiny upper wick = very bullish (his gold
    weekly example rallied and took out the highs). Mirror for bearish.
- **Only act on closed candles.** "We can only make bias and decision on candles that have formed, not candles that are
  forming." That's why a break of structure waits for the candle close.
- **A candle pattern is only one confluence, never a trade by itself**, and it must be read *with the trend*. His
  example: a bullish rejection doji on gold's daily inside a downtrend that kept breaking structure down. Don't let a
  pattern override the bias.
