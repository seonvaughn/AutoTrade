# Day 4: Trends
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=viuGHEuv0nw (29 min). Read in full from the transcript.

## Concepts
- **Why the trend matters:** markets move on momentum; once a market trends it tends to continue ("a ball rolling").
- **Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows; downtrend = lower highs and lower lows.** A trend continues until it breaks;
  a break of structure in the other direction = expect the trend to flip (detailed on Day 6).
- **Three states:** uptrend, downtrend, consolidation. In consolidation price makes no new trend extremes; it chops
  until it breaks out (his gold example: chop, then it broke the low → downtrend).
- In a downtrend the moves up are smaller than the moves down (and vice versa). Retracement depth varies, but "the
  extension always goes past the high".
- **Higher timeframes hold higher power.** A 15m break of structure against a bearish 4H/daily is noise. Use the lower
  timeframe *in confluence with* the higher trend: wait for the 15m to turn the same way as the higher timeframe.
- **Trade with the trend; don't trade retracements** (you don't know how deep they go); trade momentum. His own biggest
  recurring mistake and losing weeks: getting caught up in small timeframes and trading against his higher-timeframe bias.
- **His entry idea, previewed:** a liquidity sweep on the 30m, then a break of structure down on the 5m, while the 4H
  and daily are bearish → "we just caught the top of the next leg down". He trades sweeps and breaks only on lower
  timeframes, *in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend*.
- **You must know where price wants to go for the day** before taking intraday trades. "If you don't know where price
  wants to go for the day, you're going to lose." Pure 1-minute sweep-plus-break entries without that context are
  low probability: you could find a million of them.
- A line chart (no candle colors) makes the direction obvious on higher timeframes.
- Mentions equilibrium / premium ("we're in a premium, now we look for a trend shift on the 5 or 15 minute"), taught later.
- **Focus on one or two instruments only.**
